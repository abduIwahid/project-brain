# The 20 UX Laws — standing design checklist

Applied quietly to every frontend build. Adopted 2026-10-22 from a reel the owner shared; proven in production across a portfolio, a class resource hub and a hackathon app.

1. **Aesthetic-usability effect** — polished-looking UI is perceived as easier to use.
2. **Hick's law** — fewer, clearer choices; decision time grows with options.
3. **Fitts's law** — make important targets big and close (chunky primary buttons, ~44px+ taps).
4. **Jakob's law** — users expect your site to work like sites they already know; reuse familiar patterns.
5. **Miller's law** — chunk information (~7±2 items) into groups.
6. **Doherty threshold** — respond/feed back in under ~400ms; skeleton screens and instant state changes while loading.
7. **Law of proximity** — related items close together; unrelated items spaced apart.
8. **Law of similarity** — same-looking elements read as same-function; keep component styles consistent.
9. **Common region** — group related content inside one card/border.
10. **Visual hierarchy** — size, weight and colour must make the most important thing obviously first.
11. **Progressive disclosure** — show essentials first; advanced options behind a tap/expand.
12. **Recognition over recall** — show options and recent items instead of making users remember.
13. **Von Restorff effect** — the one different-looking element (e.g. an accent Upload pill) is the one remembered.
14. **Peak-end rule** — people judge an experience by its peak and its ending; make completions feel rewarding.
15. **Goal-gradient effect** — show progress (bars, "3 of 10 seen") to pull people to finish.
16. **Serial position effect** — first and last items in a list/nav are remembered best; put key actions there.
17. **Tesler's law** — complexity can't be deleted, only moved; absorb it in the system, not the user.
18. **Postel's law** — be liberal in what you accept (flexible inputs, forgiving search) and strict in what you output.
19. **Occam's razor** — simplest design that does the job wins; cut decorative complexity.
20. **Feedback loops** — every action gets a visible reaction (toasts, badges, state changes).
